How to Buy a SHACMAN Truck from China

A practical, configuration-first guide for importers, fleets and contractors comparing heavy trucks for overseas operation.

Short answer: Define the job and destination requirements first, compare a written technical configuration—not just a model name—and confirm commercial, inspection, shipping and warranty terms before payment.

1. Start with the operating requirement

For a dump truck

State the material, density, target payload, road surface, gradients, altitude, temperature, haul distance and preferred body dimensions.

For a tractor truck

State the trailer type, cargo, target gross combination weight, route, distance, gradients, coupling and fuel availability.

2. Confirm destination-market requirements

Before selecting a configuration, verify steering position, emission standard, dimensions, legal axle loads, lighting, tires, registration documents and any local inspection rules. The buyer or local agent should confirm the final vehicle can be registered and operated in the destination country.

3. Compare the complete specification

A model name alone is not enough. Compare the engine and rating, transmission, front and rear axles, final-drive ratio, suspension, tires, cab, fuel tank, braking system, electrical standard and optional equipment. For dump trucks, also compare body dimensions, steel thickness, tipping system and tailgate.

4. Ask for a written commercial offer

The quotation should identify the exact configuration, quantity, price basis, validity, payment terms, estimated production or availability, Incoterm, loading port and what is excluded. Shipping cost should be tied to a stated method and destination.

5. Agree on inspection and documents

Confirm the inspection scope, photos or video, chassis identification, packing, export documents and the documents needed for import clearance. Inspection arrangements and acceptance criteria should be written before shipment.

6. Confirm after-sales terms in writing

Ask what warranty terms apply to the selected vehicle, which components are covered, the claim procedure, required evidence, parts support and exclusions. Do not rely on a general website statement when the sales contract can define the actual terms.

Frequently asked questions

What information is needed for a quotation?

Destination country, truck type, quantity, application, cargo or payload, road conditions, driving side, emission requirement and preferred delivery port.

Why can the same model have different prices?

Engine, transmission, axle, tire, cab, upper-body, emissions, options, delivery terms and shipping requirements can all change the final configuration and price.

How do I reduce purchasing risk?

Use a complete signed specification, verify company and payment details independently, agree inspection and acceptance criteria, and keep commercial and warranty terms in writing.
